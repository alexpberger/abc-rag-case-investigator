title: Personal Loan Credit Policy
---

This excerpt is the personal-loan portion of the credit policy of Berger Bank. It is a fictional teaching document. It states what a file must contain before a person may consider a loan, what an application form cannot establish by itself, and who is allowed to decide. It does not describe the Hansen application, and it does not apply itself to any particular set of facts. It publishes no score cutoff, no debt-ratio pass mark, and no income floor.

# Eligibility

A personal loan may be considered when the file contains verified income, employment tenure, and documented obligations, and when a person authorised to lend has reviewed that file. The policy text alone does not approve or decline an application. A decision requires the documents, the open items, and the judgment of that person.

## Verified income and employment

Verified income is income shown on documents in the file. The figure written on the application is not verified income. Employment tenure is the time at the current employer shown on those documents. An earlier period counts only when a document in the file shows it. The applicant's statement of a start date is recorded as a statement. It is not, by itself, the tenure this section uses.

Where two documents give two start dates, or two income figures, both stay in the file. The case worker does not average them into one employment history or one monthly amount. A difference is an open item until a further document explains it.

## Documented obligations

Rent, credit cards, and other loans are taken from documents and from bureau information in the file. The application's description of those obligations is a statement, not a substitute for the documents. A sentence that an account is paid in full is recorded as that sentence. It does not replace a record that shows a balance.

The policy does not convert those records into a ratio, and it does not tell the reader which balance is acceptable. Affordability is reviewed by the person who decides, from the obligations the file actually contains. If the documents and the application do not describe the same obligations, the file says so.

# Stated and irregular income

Income that appears only on the application is stated income. Stated income does not meet the verified-income section. Overtime, a bonus, or another amount that is present in some periods and absent in others is irregular for this file until documents show it across the period the case worker has asked to see.

An irregular amount is not folded into the application's single monthly figure. The assistant does not perform that fold, and the application form does not perform it either. These limits describe what the file has not yet established. They are not an approval and they are not a decline.

# Information required before a decision

A lending decision waits until the records that bear on income, tenure, and obligations are in the file, or until a documented effort to obtain them has failed and the lender accepts the gap. For a personal loan, the file should contain the following before anyone treats the application as ready to decide. Missing items are listed as missing. They are not filled in from memory, or from what a similar application usually contains.

The file needs income documents for the recent period, the employment start date or a sourced account of earlier employment, and documents of the obligations the applicant will still have if the loan is made. It also needs an explanation of any material difference between the application and those documents, including a credit on a bank extract that the income papers do not identify. A requested record that was not supplied is recorded as not supplied. Failure to provide it does not, by itself, decide the loan. It leaves the file unfinished.

# Who decides

A lending decision is made by an authorised lender at Berger Bank. No vendor, score, ratio, or software assistant approves or declines a loan. An investigation report can arrange what the documents say, point to these sections, and list what is still missing. It is not a lending decision.

When the documents do not establish that income, tenure, and obligations are present and consistent with the application, the lender does not yet have a basis for a final decision. The policy does not require a guess. It requires a decision by a person, made on a sufficient file. Holding the file open is neither an approval nor a decline.
