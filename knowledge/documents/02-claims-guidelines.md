---

These guidelines tell investigators at Northline Example Mutual how to build a water-damage file. They are internal instructions. They do not change the policy, and they do not authorise the investigator to pay or deny a claim. The Hale file is the kind of file these instructions describe: a report of water in the home, a plumbing observation, and an unfinished set of records.

# Purpose

The investigation exists to collect and organise facts a human adjuster can use. The investigator identifies the policy sections that might apply, records what each document actually says, and keeps supporting observations separate from observations that point the other way. The investigator does not convert an incomplete file into a coverage outcome. A sentence that the loss "must be" covered, or "must be" excluded, is a conclusion, and conclusions are reserved for the adjuster.

# Weighing technical observations

Technical notes from a plumber are evidence about the condition of the pipe and what was seen on the visit. They are not a coverage decision. Investigators quote the observations and keep the plumber's limits in view. If the note describes corrosion, scale, pinholes, or staining, those words go in the file as observations. They are not rewritten as "gradual cause confirmed" unless the author wrote that conclusion, which a careful inspection note usually does not.

An observation can bear on more than one policy question. Staining can matter to seepage or leakage over time. An active release on the day of the visit can matter to when water was present. Record both. If the technical note and the claim form do not answer the same question, request the missing record rather than averaging the two accounts into one cause.

# Records required before a decision

A coverage decision waits until the records that bear on cause and timing are in the file, or until a documented effort to obtain them has failed and the adjuster accepts the gap. For a plumbing leak, the file should contain the items in the following sections before anyone treats the claim as ready to decide. Missing items are listed in the open-items note. They are not filled in from memory or from what a similar claim usually looks like.

## Maintenance history

The file needs the maintenance history for the plumbing that is said to have leaked: invoices, service notes, or a statement of when the piping was last worked on. A statement that there has been no maintenance is recorded as a statement. Absence of invoices is recorded as an absence, not as proof that maintenance never happened and not as proof that it was neglected.

## Age of the pipe

The file needs the age of the pipe, or a documented statement that the age is unknown. Age can come from building papers, a prior invoice, or a sourced statement. A guess from the look of the metal is labelled as a guess. Age by itself does not establish gradual deterioration, and it does not establish a sudden discharge. If no source is in the file, the open-items note says the age of the pipe is unknown.

## When the leak was noticed

The file needs a clear account of when the leak was noticed, and of whether any dampness, stain, or drip was noticed before that time. Keep differing dates side by side. "I first saw water on this date" is not the same sentence as "no water escaped before this date." When the file has only the first sentence, the timing is incomplete.

## Photographs and moisture readings

The file needs dated photographs of the pipe, the escape point, and the damaged finishes, plus any moisture readings with their location and value. If the claim says photographs were taken and they are not in the file, the open-items note says so. A moisture reading shows that material is wet. It does not, by itself, show whether the wetting was one discharge or a longer period. A reading that was never taken is not described as if it had been.

# Insufficient evidence

When the records in this part are missing, or when the observations conflict and nothing further resolves them, the evidence is insufficient for a coverage decision. Report that state, list the gaps, and recommend the next records to request. Do not fill the gap by presuming the loss was sudden, and do not fill it by presuming the loss was gradual. Holding the file open is not a denial and not a payment. The adjuster sees the gap and decides what to do with it.
