This report was prepared by R. Okonkwo of Example Harbour Plumbing for Jordan Hale at 18 Paperbirch Court, Example Harbour. The visit was on 6 March 2026. The report describes the pipe that was inspected and what was visible on that visit. It is a technical note for the customer and for Berger Insurance Company. It does not interpret the homeowners policy and it does not state a cause in the language of coverage.

# Inspection visit

The plumber attended on 6 March 2026, three days after the customer said the water was found. The angle stop under the kitchen sink was closed on arrival. The cabinet floor was damp, and standing water had already been removed. The inspection was visual, without opening the wall or cutting out the pipe. No moisture meter reading was written down. The supply line was not replaced on this visit. It remained in place, isolated at the angle stop, after a brief opening to observe it.

# Pipe condition

The pipe in question is a cold-water supply line serving the kitchen sink, running from the angle stop to the faucet connection. The plumber described the visible run as original-looking copper, consistent with the rest of the under-sink plumbing, and did not state a year of installation. No stamp or label with a date was found on the visible pipe. The remarks below are limited to the length that could be seen without opening the wall.

## Corrosion and scale

The exterior of the visible copper showed scattered green corrosion product, heavier in a band near the lower bend. Scale at the faucet connector was built up rather than a film that would wipe away. The surface around the lower bend was rough. The report records that corrosion and scale were visible on 6 March 2026. It does not date when either condition began, and it contains no earlier photograph of this pipe.

## Pinhole cluster

On the lower bend, the plumber observed a cluster of small pinholes. Three openings were visible without magnification, close together, with thin and irregular edges. The note describes a group of pinholes. It does not describe a split, a tear, or a single large opening, and it does not say what process produced them or when they formed. These pinholes are where water left the pipe when the angle stop was opened.

## Staining

The cabinet floor under the bend showed a brown stain extending beyond the area still damp on the visit, darker near the bend and lighter toward the toe kick. The plumber wrote that the staining was consistent with earlier moisture, and did not date that moisture. The customer said during the visit that the cabinet had looked clean. The report records both the stain and that remark, and it does not decide between them.

# Active release on the inspection date

When the angle stop was opened, water left the pipe through the pinhole cluster as a steady trickle, not a spray. Closing the stop stopped the trickle. That active release was observed on 6 March 2026. The report does not say the release began that day, and it does not say it had been continuous beforehand. The stop was closed on arrival, so the visit does not show whether water was leaving the pipe on 3 March.

# Limits of this report

This report does not conclude that the pinholes formed slowly, and it does not conclude that they formed in a single sudden event. It does not use a cause label for the loss. It does not estimate how long the cabinet had been damp before 6 March 2026. It does not state the age of the pipe as a number. The observations are the corrosion and scale, the pinhole cluster, the staining consistent with earlier moisture, and the active release seen when the stop was opened.

Recommended work, as plumbing work only, is replacement of the visible supply line. That recommendation is about restoring a water supply. It is not a statement of why the openings are in the pipe, and it is not an instruction to an insurer. Any question about policy wording is outside the scope of the inspection. The plumber's role ended with the observations and the repair recommendation above.
