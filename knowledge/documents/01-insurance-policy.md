title: Homeowners Policy Water Damage Provisions
---

This excerpt is the water-damage portion of the Homeowners Policy issued by Northline Example Mutual. It is a fictional teaching document. It states what the policy covers, what it excludes, and who is allowed to make a coverage decision. It does not describe the Hale loss, and it does not apply itself to any particular set of facts.

# Coverage

The policy pays for direct physical loss to the dwelling caused by a covered peril, subject to the exclusions and the duties below. Payment is made to the named insured after a coverage decision. The policy text alone does not decide whether a particular loss is covered. A decision requires the facts of the loss, the investigation file, and the judgment of the person authorised to decide.

## Sudden and accidental discharge or overflow

The policy covers direct physical loss caused by sudden and accidental discharge or overflow of water from a plumbing system inside the dwelling. The resulting damage to building finishes and to covered property can be a covered loss when this section applies. A slow drip the insured had already noticed, or damage that accumulated while a leak went unattended, is not what this section describes. The insured's choice of the word sudden is not proof that the discharge was sudden. Whether a reported event meets the section is a question of fact.

# Exclusions

The coverage grant is limited by the exclusions in this part. An exclusion applies when the facts show that the loss falls within it, even if some of the resulting water damage looks similar to damage that another section might otherwise cover. Investigators gather the facts. They do not cross the exclusion out because a claim form uses the word sudden.

## Gradual deterioration

The policy does not cover loss caused by gradual deterioration of plumbing materials. Wear, corrosion, and other slow change in a pipe, fitting, or joint are not a covered peril. If the discharge of water results from gradual deterioration, the resulting water damage is excluded even when the water is first noticed on a single day.

This exclusion is about the cause of the escape. It is not limited to damage that an occupant watched happening over many days. A pipe can fail on a Tuesday because the metal has been deteriorating for years. The exclusion still applies when the facts show that gradual deterioration caused the escape. The exclusion does not, by itself, prove what happened in any individual home. It only states the rule that applies when that cause is established.

## Seepage and leakage over time

The policy does not cover loss caused by seepage or leakage over time. Repeated wetting, staining that developed across more than one incident, and damage from water that escaped in small amounts over a period of time fall under this exclusion. The exclusion applies whether or not the insured noticed the earlier wetting.

A single observation of standing water does not tell the reader whether the escape was a one-time discharge or the latest sign of leakage over time. Moisture marks, prior staining, and the condition of the pipe are relevant because they can show earlier escape. They are not a substitute for this section. This section is the exclusion. The inspection report, if any, is the evidence.

# Duties after a loss

After a loss, the insured must give prompt notice, protect the property from further damage, and show the damaged property. The insured must also provide records and documents that Northline Example Mutual reasonably requests, including maintenance history and any earlier reports of leaks. Failure to provide a requested record does not, by itself, decide coverage. It does leave the file incomplete.

The insured cooperates and does not decide whether an exclusion applies. Photographs, invoices, and maintenance notes are part of the file when they exist. If they were requested and not supplied, the file says so.

# Who decides coverage

A coverage decision is made by an authorised adjuster at Northline Example Mutual. No vendor, inspector, or software assistant decides whether the policy pays. An investigation report can arrange the facts, point to these sections, and list what is still missing. It is not a coverage determination.

The adjuster may pay, deny, or hold the claim only after considering the whole file. When the facts do not show whether the escape was a sudden and accidental discharge or the result of gradual deterioration, seepage, or leakage over time, the adjuster does not yet have a basis for a final decision. The policy does not require a guess. It requires a decision by a person, made on a sufficient file.
